Identity & Belonging
Q31
To what degree does the ad signal an IN-GROUP — a sense of "us", people united by a shared identity, lifestyle, values, or worldview that the ad appears to be speaking to or about?
The group doesn't need to be visually obvious — an in-group can be defined by shared beliefs, cultural values, or a way of seeing the world. What matters is whether the ad signals "this is for people like us." Code based on whether an audience member could recognise the in-group — not whether you personally identify with it.
1→2: do the signals suggest some shared values or identity but lack focus or coordination (1), or do multiple elements — language, setting, scenario, music, references — all point at the same group, creating a clear sense of "us" (2)?
Required Evidence
Q32
To what degree does the ad suggest or imply an OUT-GROUP — a sense of "them", people who fall outside the world or perspective the ad is speaking to?
1→2: is the out-group merely the logical flipside of the in-group — an implied "them" that follows automatically from the "us" (1) — or does the ad actively characterise, show, or name those who are "not this", making the contrast explicit (2)?

Overall Strategy (complete last)
Q49
What is the DOMINANT strategy driving this ad? (Synthesis — complete after all other questions)
Joke/Irony vs Emotional Linear: remove the humour — does the ad still work? Yes = Emotional Linear with humour on top. No = Joke/Irony. Functional RTB vs Problem-Solution: making a claim about the product (Functional RTB), or solving the protagonist's problem (Problem-Solution)? Shared Values vs Emotional Elliptical: can you state the ad's position in one declarative sentence the brand would sign? Yes = Shared Values. No = Emotional Elliptical. Emotional Linear vs Problem-Solution: does the brand drive the resolution? Brand drives = Problem-Solution. Character resolves own arc = Emotional Linear.
